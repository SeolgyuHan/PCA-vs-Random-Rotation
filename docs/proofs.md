# Proofs and derivations

Full proofs of the results that the paper states with a sketch or a pointer. Numbering (Lemma 1, Propositions 1–3, Assumptions 1–5, Sections III-A to III-D) follows the paper. $\sigma_i^2(U)$ is the variance of component $i$ in the orthogonal basis $U$, $\lambda$ the eigenvalues of the covariance $\Sigma$ of the direction $u$, $\bar{\lambda}$ their mean, $\mathrm{AM}$ and $\mathrm{GM}$ the arithmetic and geometric means, $\mathrm{CG} = \bar{\lambda}/\mathrm{GM}(\lambda)$ the coding gain, and $c_0(U)$ the quantizer constant of Section III-A, and $\kappa$ the constant of Assumption 5 ($E[\tilde{q}\tilde{q}^\top] = \kappa\Sigma$).

## 1. Lemma 1 (optimal allocation), full proof

**Lemma 1.** Under Assumptions 1 and 5 with continuous bits, for any orthogonal basis $U$, the allocation that minimizes the score error $J(U, b)$ subject to $\sum_i b_i = Bd$ and $b_i \ge 0$ is $b_i = \max(0, \log_2 \sigma_i^2(U) + c)$, where $c$ is the unique constant that meets the budget.

*Proof.* Let $w_i = c_0(U)\,\sigma_i^4(U) > 0$, so that $J \propto f(b) = \sum_i w_i 4^{-b_i}$. Each term is strictly convex in $b_i$, so $f$ is strictly convex; the feasible set is convex, so the minimizer is unique and is characterized by the KKT conditions. For the Lagrangian $f(b) + \mu(\sum_i b_i - Bd) - \sum_i \nu_i b_i$, stationarity reads $-\ln 4\, w_i 4^{-b_i} + \mu - \nu_i = 0$, with $\nu_i \ge 0$ and $\nu_i b_i = 0$. Let $\theta := \mu/\ln 4$. If $b_i > 0$, then $\nu_i = 0$ and $w_i 4^{-b_i} = \theta$, i.e., $b_i = \tfrac{1}{2}\log_2(w_i/\theta) = \log_2 \sigma_i^2 + c$ with $c = \tfrac{1}{2}\log_2(c_0/\theta)$ common to all components. If $b_i = 0$, then $\nu_i = \ln 4\,(\theta - w_i) \ge 0$, i.e., $w_i \le \theta$, which is $\log_2 \sigma_i^2 + c \le 0$. Together, $b_i = \max(0, \log_2 \sigma_i^2 + c)$. Since $\sum_i \max(0, \log_2 \sigma_i^2 + c)$ is continuous and increasing in $c$, the budget fixes $c$ uniquely. If every component is active, $c = B - \log_2 \mathrm{GM}(\sigma^2)$ and every term equals $c_0\,\mathrm{GM}(\sigma^2)^2\,4^{-B}$, so $J = 4\kappa E[R^2]\,c_0(U)\,d\,\mathrm{GM}(\sigma^2(U))^2\,4^{-B}$. $\square$

*Integer bits.* The experiments floor the continuous solution, add the missing bits one each to the active components with the largest fractional parts, and cap a component at 24 bits (Section IV; `pcafair/codec.py`, `alpha_alloc`).

*Contrast with the reconstruction error.* With terms $c_0\sigma_i^2 4^{-b_i}$ the same argument gives $b_i = \max(0, \tfrac{1}{2}\log_2 \sigma_i^2 + c)$, the allocation of transform coding (Goyal, 2001); the query weight $E[\tilde{q}_i^2] \propto \sigma_i^2$ (Assumption 5) doubles the slope.

## 2. The dither facts used by Proposition 2

What Section III-A cites from Schuchman (1964) and the generalized Bussgang decomposition (Demir and Björnson, 2021), written out for this quantizer.

**Claim.** For a component with at least 2 bits and cell width $\Delta$, the dithered reconstruction $\check{u}$ satisfies $\check{u} - u = \beta(u) + \omega$ for every input $u$, where $\omega$ is uniform on $[-\Delta/2, \Delta/2)$ given $u$, and $\beta(u)$ is a closed-form function of $u$ that is nonzero only within half a cell of the range ends or outside the range. With the gain $g = 1 + \mathrm{Cov}(\beta(u), u)/\mathrm{Var}(u)$ and the offset $o$ that makes the mean error zero, $\hat{u} = (\check{u} - o)/g$ satisfies that $\hat{u} - u - \omega/g$ has mean zero and is uncorrelated with $u$.

*Proof.* Let the range be $[\ell, h]$, the dither $\zeta \sim \mathcal{U}[-\Delta/2, \Delta/2)$, and $\check{u} = Q(u + \zeta) - \zeta$, where $Q$ maps to cell centres and clamps to the end cells. (a) If $u \in [\ell + \Delta/2,\ h - \Delta/2]$, then $u + \zeta$ stays in the range, so $\check{u} - u = Q(u+\zeta) - (u+\zeta)$, which under Schuchman's condition (a dither uniform over one cell) is $\mathcal{U}[-\Delta/2, \Delta/2)$ and independent of $u$; here $\beta(u) = 0$. (b) If $u$ lies within half a cell of an end or outside the range, e.g. $u \ge h - \Delta/2$, then $u + \zeta \ge h - \Delta$, the code is always the end cell, and $\check{u} - u = (h - \Delta/2 - u) + (-\zeta)$: $\beta(u) = h - \Delta/2 - u$ is a function of $u$ and $\omega = -\zeta$ is uniform and independent of $u$; the lower end is symmetric. The second part is a generalized Bussgang decomposition: $\hat{u} - u - \omega/g = ((1 - g)\,u + \beta(u) - o)/g$, whose covariance with $u$ is $((1 - g)\mathrm{Var}(u) + \mathrm{Cov}(\beta, u))/g = 0$ by the definition of $g$, and whose mean is zero by that of $o$. For 1-bit components the second part holds with $\omega = 0$. $\square$

## 3. Proposition 1 (the allocation decides the best basis), parts (i) and (ii)

*Proof.* (i) With uniform bits $b_i = B$, $J = 4\kappa E[R^2]\,c_0\,4^{-B}\sum_i \sigma_i^4(U)$, so $F(U) = \mathrm{AM}(\sigma^4(U))$. The vector $\sigma^2(U)$ is the diagonal of $U^\top \Sigma U$, and by the Schur–Horn theorem the diagonal of a symmetric matrix is majorized by its eigenvalues $\lambda$ (Marshall, Olkin, and Arnold, *Inequalities: Theory of Majorization and Its Applications*, 2011). Since $x \mapsto \sum_i x_i^2$ is Schur-convex, $\sum_i \sigma_i^4(U) \le \sum_i \lambda_i^2$, with equality for PCA. Conversely, $(\bar{\lambda}, \dots, \bar{\lambda})$ is majorized by every vector with the same sum, so $\sum_i \sigma_i^4(U) \ge d\bar{\lambda}^2$, and Schur–Horn guarantees an orthogonal basis with this diagonal (a variance-equalizing basis). The ratio of the two ends is $\mathrm{AM}(\lambda^2)/\mathrm{AM}(\lambda)^2 = 1 + \mathrm{CV}^2$. (ii) By Lemma 1 with every component active, $F(U) = \mathrm{GM}(\sigma^2(U))^2$. Hadamard's inequality gives $\det \Sigma = \det(U^\top \Sigma U) \le \prod_i \sigma_i^2(U)$, so $\mathrm{GM}(\sigma^2(U)) \ge \mathrm{GM}(\lambda)$, with equality for PCA, where $U^\top \Sigma U$ is diagonal. $\square$

## 4. The Haar deficit $\delta$ (Section III-C)

*Derivation.* Each column $\xi$ of a dense Haar rotation is uniform on the unit sphere and $\sigma_i^2 = \xi^\top \Sigma \xi$. By rotation invariance take $\Sigma = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda)$, so $\xi^\top \Sigma \xi = \sum_k \lambda_k \xi_k^2$; with the moments of a uniform unit vector, $E[\xi_k^4] = 3/(d(d+2))$ and $E[\xi_k^2 \xi_l^2] = 1/(d(d+2))$ for $k \ne l$, $E[(\xi^\top \Sigma \xi)^2] = (\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)^2 + 2\,\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma^2))/(d(d+2))$. Substituting $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma) = d\bar{\lambda}$ and $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma^2) = d\bar{\lambda}^2(1 + \mathrm{CV}^2)$ gives $E[\sigma_i^4] = \bar{\lambda}^2(1 + \delta)$ with $\delta = 2\,\mathrm{CV}^2/(d + 2)$. With uniform bits $J_{\mathrm{rot}} = 4\kappa E[R^2]\,c_0(U_{\mathrm{rot}})\,4^{-B}\,d\,\bar{\lambda}^2(1 + \delta)$, and by Lemma 1 $J_{\mathrm{PCA}} = 4\kappa E[R^2]\,c_0(U_{\mathrm{PCA}})\,d\,\mathrm{GM}(\lambda)^2 4^{-B}$, so the ratio is $\mathrm{CG}^2(1 + \delta)\,S$ with $S = c_0(U_{\mathrm{rot}})/c_0(U_{\mathrm{PCA}})$. $\square$

## 5. Proposition 2 (a data-independent interval), full proof

*Proof.* Fix the query and split, over all components, $\langle \tilde{q}, \check{u} - u \rangle = \sum_i \tilde{q}_i \omega_i + \langle \tilde{q}, r(x) \rangle$, where the sum runs over the components with at least 2 bits and $\check{u}$ keeps the dithered reconstruction before gain correction for them, the (gain-corrected) split level for the 1-bit components, and the mean $m_i$ for the components without bits. By the facts of Section 2, the $\omega_i$ of the components with at least 2 bits are independent (a separate dither per component), have mean zero and satisfy $|\omega_i| \le \Delta_i/2$. Bernstein's inequality for $X_i = \tilde{q}_i \omega_i$, with $V = \sum_i \tilde{q}_i^2 \Delta_i^2/12$ and $M = \max_i |\tilde{q}_i| \Delta_i/2$, gives $\Pr[|\sum_i X_i| \ge \tau] \le 2\exp(-\tau^2/(2V + 2M\tau/3))$ (see, e.g., Vershynin, *High-Dimensional Probability*, 2018); setting the right side to $\eta$ and solving for $\tau$ gives $\tau_\eta(q)$. The rest, $r(x)$, collects $\beta$ for the dithered components, the errors of the 1-bit components, and, for each component without bits, the error $m_i - u_i$ of its reconstruction as the mean; it is zero outside $D$, the components coded to an end cell, the 1-bit components, and the components without bits (Section 2), so Cauchy–Schwarz gives $|\langle \tilde{q}, r(x) \rangle| \le \|\tilde{q}_D\|_2\,\rho(x) \le \|\tilde{q}_D\|_2\,\hat{\rho}(x)$. Adding the two parts, the inequality of Proposition 2 holds with probability at least $1 - \eta$. $\square$

*From the inner product to the score.* With $\check{s}(x) = R^2 - 2R\langle \tilde{q}, \check{u}\rangle$ and $s(x) = R^2 - 2R\langle \tilde{q}, u\rangle$ (Eq. (3) of the paper), $\check{s}(x) - s(x) = -2R\langle \tilde{q}, \check{u} - u\rangle$, so on the same event $|s(x) - \check{s}(x)| \le 2R\,(\tau_\eta(q) + \|\tilde{q}_D\|_2\,\hat{\rho}(x))$. The ranking estimate $\hat{s}(x) = R^2 - 2R\langle \tilde{q}, \hat{u}\rangle$ (Eq. (5)) satisfies $\hat{s}(x) = \check{s}(x) + 2R\langle \tilde{q}, \check{u} - \hat{u}\rangle$, where $\hat{u}_i - \check{u}_i = ((1 - g_i)\check{u}_i - o_i)/g_i$ for the dithered components and $0$ otherwise (the 1-bit levels are already gain-corrected; the components without bits are the mean), a quantity computed from the code, the shared dither, and the fixed gains and offsets. The coverage of the score interval is therefore that of Proposition 2.

The measurement of the interval over all components (coverage at nominal levels 95, 99, and 99.9% over the first 20,000 database vectors, the first 200 queries, dither seeds 0–4, budgets 1–8, and residual codes of 2–4 bits) is `experiments/guarantee/guarantee.py`; its output is `results/guarantee/`.
